# Knowledge Engines

## Scope

This analysis uses only `TRIET_LY_GAP_REPORT.md`, `TRIET_LY_BRAIN_READINESS.md`, `TRIET_LY_DOMAIN_EXPANSION.md`, `TRIET_LY_STRATEGIC_ROADMAP.md`, and `TRIET_LY_TOP_100_PRIORITIES.md`. “Engine” means a repeatable mechanism that could generate, validate, connect, or revise knowledge; it does not assert that such a mechanism is currently operating.

### Leverage scale

- **High:** can improve many downstream knowledge objects while strengthening evidence or governance.
- **Medium:** useful, but dependent on owners, context-specific criteria, or consistent inputs.
- **Low:** narrow, speculative, or unable to produce reliable new knowledge without other engines.

## Engine dependencies

### FACT

- The reports describe evidence review, authorization, audit lineage, verified learning, failure/risk separation, and knowledge-atomization as reusable concepts.
- Candidate procedures and prompts are not validated defaults.
- The current materials do not establish actual runtime mechanisms or measured effectiveness.

### INFERENCE

- The highest-leverage engine is a governed review loop linking evidence-backed research, decision review, audit, and verified learning. Any one in isolation is less useful: evidence without decisions may not change knowledge; decisions without audit cannot be reconstructed; audit without review does not ensure learning.
- Failure analysis has high potential leverage but should remain dormant as an incident-derived engine until verified incident evidence is available.
- The next investment should establish ownership, source/evidence criteria, and one measurable review path before pursuing automation or volume.

### UNKNOWN

- Which engines are already active in practice.
- Relative yield, cost, error rate, and downstream impact of each.
- Whether any engine can improve knowledge quality consistently across domains.

## Direct answers

### FACT

The source reports identify ownership, target criteria, evidence standards, audit controls, and outcome measures as open gaps.

### INFERENCE

- **Largest growth driver:** repeated evidence-backed review that converts outcomes into approved, traceable knowledge.
- **Largest competitive advantage candidate:** trusted provenance and a verified learning history, if accumulated and shown to improve decisions.
- **Most deserving investment:** establish a human-owned, auditable research → decision → outcome review process.
- **Stop/defer investment:** expanding the catalog, activating untested prompts/SOPs, or building autonomous learning/multi-agent structures without validated need and evidence.

### UNKNOWN

Whether these choices would yield a competitive advantage or measurable growth; no comparative data is available in the authorized sources.
